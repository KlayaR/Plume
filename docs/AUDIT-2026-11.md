# Plume audit, November 2026

Version 0.45.0 (commit `ea4693c`), audited on 10 November 2026. No code was changed. This file and the samples in `docs/audit-samples/` are the only additions; neither is committed.

## How to read this

Each finding carries a tag:

- **[R]** reproduced or measured during the audit.
- **[C]** read in the code, not run.
- **[J]** my judgement; you may reasonably disagree.

What was done:

- **Code read in full:** the Rust side, the data layer and store, every AI module and prompt, the context engine, the Plan screens and logic, the editor, the Review panel, the Bible editor, Settings, backups and snapshots.
- **Checks run:** 215 unit tests pass, `tsc` passes, the French-strings and version checks pass. With unused-code checks on, 14 declarations are unused.
- **Live checks** in the browser mock (`/?mock`, `/?mock=long`): save on close, what a request contains, the French scorecard, typing speed, tracked changes, the main screens.
- **Real Claude calls:** five scenes written through the `claude` CLI (2.1.319) with Plume's exact flags, on the sample book. Seven calls on the `sonnet` alias at medium effort, about 40,000 output tokens of your subscription. Prompts and replies are in `docs/audit-samples/`.
- **Your Aurors book:** counts and settings only, read without opening any text.
- **Competitors:** web searches on 10 November 2026 plus what I already knew. Most pages found were third-party reviews, and I did not use the products.

The request asked about databases, authentication, APIs and SaaS scaling. Plume has none of these: it is a single-user desktop app on plain files. Where the question has a local equivalent (file safety, key storage, the CLI integration), I answer that instead.

## Summary

Plume's foundations are good: plain Markdown folders, a small Rust side, nothing deleted, tracked changes instead of silent rewrites, and a bible model richer than most competitors'. The work of the last four weeks went into breadth. About fifty AI operations and a dozen analysis screens now sit on an AI core that under-supplies context and over-specifies style, and on a few lifecycle bugs that can lose work.

Six things need fixing before anything else, all but one reproduced:

1. Switching books can lose saves that were still waiting.
2. A scene written from its plan is sent without the bible entries of its cast and place; in the real runs this produced contradictions with the bible every time.
3. The automatic length follow-up adds a second ending and invents plot.
4. The French scorecard reports passives and adverbs that are not there, and sends them to Claude as things to rewrite.
5. A failed save does not stop the window from closing.
6. Tracked changes and proposals disappear when you leave the scene.

After that, the gains are in what Claude is given (story memory, the cast, your approved prose) and in removing mechanisms that work against the text (duplicated rules, the ban list in the prompt, metrics used as targets), not in new features.

## 1. Technical architecture and code quality

### What is sound

- **Books are plain folders** of Markdown with YAML headers; unknown keys are kept (`lib/bible.ts`). No lock-in.
- **File safety by design:** temp-file-then-rename writes, a trash instead of deletion, conflict copies when a synced file changed elsewhere (`lib/project.ts` `guarded`), a lock file, daily backups, per-scene history, undo for the Plan and the Bible.
- **The CLI integration is correct.** Every flag passed in `src-tauri/src/lib.rs` `run_claude` exists in CLI 2.1.319. Measured input sizes are consistent with the documented overhead of about 830 tokens, and the system prompt is read back from the cache on a second call (1,987 tokens in the follow-up runs). [R]
- **Pure logic is separated** in `src/lib/` and tested: 215 tests in 70 groups.
- **The code is readable.** Files are short, comments say why, naming is consistent.

### Problems

**Data safety**

- **Switching books loses pending saves. [R]** `store.ts` `close()` calls `flush()` without waiting, then sets `book: null`. Writes run one after the other; from the second on they find no book and return without writing. Test: two scenes and a chapter title changed, then `close()`: the first scene was saved, the other two changes were not, and the status stayed on "Saving".
- **A failed save does not stop the close. [C]** `App.tsx` awaits `flush()` on window close but ignores a failure. The warning is a nine-second toast plus the bottom bar, which fades to 8 % opacity while you type (focus mode, on by default).
- **Review work lives only in the editor's memory. [R]** Tracked changes went from 2 to 0 after a visit to the Plan; a waiting proposal vanished on a scene switch in Scene view. Nothing warns you.
- **Backups can drop to one copy. [C]** `write_backup` deletes everything older than 14 days. After two weeks away, the next open leaves only that day's zip. Backups are keyed by the book's title, so a rename starts a new folder: your Aurors book appears to have two, one per title. They sit on the same disk and leave out history and snapshots.
- **A broken YAML header is read as empty. [C]** `splitFrontmatter` swallows the error; the next save rewrites the header from defaults, and the scene's plan card, comments and id are gone. Rare, but silent.
- **No flush to disk before the rename. [C]** A power cut during a save can leave an empty file. History softens this.

**Performance**

- **Typing cost grows with the book. [R]** Main-thread work per keystroke in the dev build: 8 ms on the sample book, about 80 ms on 180,000 words, 131 ms in Review mode. 23 ms of it is recounting the words of all 120 scenes for the left pane on every key; the Review reports cost 31 ms per scene and 92 ms per chapter, also on every key. A release build is faster, but the growth is the same.
- **Each "snapshot first" stores the whole book**, and the snapshot list reads every snapshot in full. [C]
- **Commands are synchronous**, which in Tauri runs them on the main thread as far as I know; a book load is two calls per file. Not measured in the installed app. [C]

**Structure**

- **AI code is spread out. [C]** 34 call sites in 16 files, each with its prompt in two languages, each parsing JSON with the same heuristic (`ai.ts` `parseJSON`).
- **Model choice is fragmented and partly dead. [R]** Five mechanisms coexist: per-action settings in the book, the Assistant's model, the Chat's model, presets, cheap mode. The per-action screen is gone (`PresetSelect`, `setSetting`, `addClaude`, `ACTIONS` are unused), so effort stays at "low" for most prose actions with no way to change it. Claude presets can be edited in Settings but nothing reads them. Planning, editing, checking and agent calls are fixed on Sonnet in the code.
- **Three implementations of "Claude's edits as tracked changes"** with different matching, guards and context (section 6).
- **One store of 817 lines** holds the book, navigation, saving, undo and usage. It works; the save lifecycle inside it is where the first P0 lives, and nothing tests it.

**Security**

- `"csp": null`, and the commands accept any path and any program (`read_file_base64`, `write_file_base64`, `run_claude`'s `exe`). I found no place where Plume renders outside text as HTML, so this is hardening, not an open hole. [C]
- The OpenRouter and LanguageTool keys are in the web view's local storage in plain text. Acceptable for a personal tool. [C]
- Agent tasks get read tools without being confined to the book folder; the CLI now has `--restricted` for that. [C]

**Tests and practice**

- Tests cover `src/lib` only: nothing for the store, the editor state, or the AI flows. No Rust tests, no component tests, no linter (the `eslint-disable` comments refer to a tool that is not installed).
- `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` says the bible goes in the system prompt; the code puts it in the message.

### Answers

- **Clean, efficient, robust?** Clean yes. Robust at rest, less so at the edges of a session (closing, switching, navigating).
- **Decisions that will hurt as Plume grows?** State that exists only in an editor view; AI tasks without a common shape; the whole book re-rendered on every keystroke.
- **Overengineering?** Not in the code. In the product: five model mechanisms, three line-edit paths, four places for setups and payoffs.
- **Simpler ways?** One function for "ask Claude for JSON"; one edit engine; one card editor; one model policy.
- **Hidden problems?** The ones tagged [R] above.
- **Refactor, rewrite, remove?** No rewrite. Fix the save lifecycle, consolidate the AI layer, delete the dead model settings.

### What is fast

| Task | Steps |
|---|---|
| Continue the scene and keep it | Ctrl+Enter, Tab |
| Rewrite a passage | Select, "Rewrite" on the bar, Tab or "Change by change" |
| Open any scene, entry or action | Ctrl+K, a few letters |
| Add a name to the bible | Hover the underlined name, add |
| New scene, new chapter | Enter twice, Enter again |

### What costs clicks or gets lost

| Task | Steps today | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Update the story memory | Plan, Analysis, Dashboard, "Update" | Four clicks, hidden in beginner mode, and nothing tells you it matters. |
| Raise the effort for drafting | Not possible | The screen was removed. |
| Go through a chapter edit | One mouse click per change | No keyboard, no "accept the rest", and the changes vanish if you leave. |
| Have Claude interview you on the story | Hover the story header to see the button | The main Claude actions on story, chapters and cards appear only on hover. [R] |
| Decide how Claude writes | Settings, Voice: seven inputs in four sections | Prompt, style guide, voice description, scene rules, taste, word lists and excerpts all say "how to write". |
| Tag a scene with a story line | Board inspector or the tracker grid | Not possible from the outline card you are editing. |

### Other observations

- **First open of a book shows three things at once:** a nine-point tour, a tip card and a toast. [R]
- **One toast at a time:** progress messages replace errors.
- **Focus mode fades the bar that carries the save status.**
- **Beginner mode hides the Analysis tabs,** where the button that refreshes summaries is.
- **A proposal is all or nothing** (Tab or Esc), except rewrites.

### Concrete changes

1. Make summaries automatic, so nobody has to find the Dashboard.
2. Show the Claude buttons on the story header, chapters and cards without hover (dimmed is fine).
3. Tracked changes: keys for next, accept and reject; "accept the rest in this scene"; keep them when you leave.
4. One card editor, opened the same way from Cards, List, Board and the manuscript.
5. One "Voice" page: your samples, one text of instructions, the checks.
6. First open: the tip card only; the tour stays in the menu.
7. A save failure gets a bar that stays until it is resolved.

## 4. AI architecture, prompts and writing intelligence

### What a request contains

Assembled for "write the scene from its plan" on the sample book, default settings [R]:

- **System prompt, 4,883 bytes:** 6 base rules, 5 scene rules, 10 preset bullets (each with several instructions), 35 banned phrases, the style guide. No sample of the author's prose, because none exists yet.
- **Message, 3,049 bytes:** three bible entries, one line of the previous scene, the plan card, 5 more craft bullets, a scope block.
- **Measured:** 3,887 tokens in, 6,086 out. The reply is the expensive part; the context is the cheap part.

### Five real runs

Same scene card each time: four beats, cast Élise and Marc, place Valombre. Bible: Marc is "captain of the guard, loyal, in love with Élise without saying it"; Valombre is "a town burned fifteen years ago, still in ruins to the north"; Élise "lit the fire of Valombre as a child". One reply per setting: these are illustrations, not statistics. Files are in `docs/audit-samples/`.

| Run | Request | Length asked | First reply | Follow-up | Against the bible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plume's default | at least 3,000 | 2,391 words | Triggered: +619 | Valombre "to the south"; Marc was twelve, with his mother, never a captain; invented Bertrand de Saulx, Roche-Haute, Saint-Aubin |
| 2 | Default, your book's length | at least 5,000 | 3,923 words | Triggered: +949 | "Eleven years"; the follow-up makes the fire "not only" hers and Valombre rebuilt; invented Brenn, Hélouin |
| 3 | Base rules only, no preset | at least 3,000 | 2,799 words | Not triggered | Marc a stable boy of fourteen; "ten years"; Valombre a house |
| 4a | Proposed prompt with cast and place entries | none | 708 words | None | Nothing found: captain, the guard's key, fifteen years, the north road |
| 4b | Same, target 2,500 to 3,500 | target | 1,855 words | None | Nothing found: the captain's oath, the relief he set himself, a ruined town, fifteen years |

What the runs show:

- **Missing entries cause contradictions. [R]** Every run without Marc's and Valombre's entries contradicted the bible; both runs with them did not, and used them ("Je suis capitaine d'une garde qui n'a rien vu").
- **"Never invent proper nouns" does not hold when the model lacks what it needs. [R]** Runs 1 and 2 invented at least five names under that rule.
- **The follow-up writes past the end. [R]** In runs 1 and 2 the first reply had played all four beats and closed on a hook. The follow-up, told "the scene isn't finished", replayed the sword-heats beat, added a third witness, and ended on a second cliffhanger. Both follow-ups invented the same one: a voice saying her childhood name.
- **The ban list misfires. [R]** Run 1 used "tapisserie", the first word of the list, for an actual tapestry. The list also bans everyday phrases ("le poids de", "un mélange de", "retint son souffle").
- **Some habits belong to the model, not the prompt. [R]** Across runs with different prompts: the wall "higher than she thought" opens three of five; "plus jeune… plus vieux"; a face that is "nu"; ladders of "— Oui."; "quelque chose qu'elle ne…"; "comme on…"; about one simile every 220 words whether or not a rule limits them. No instruction removed these.
- **Without a floor the scene is short. [R]** 708 words with no length, 1,855 with a target of 2,500 to 3,500. Length needs to be asked for, but as guidance.
- **Claude's French is not error-free. [R]** Run 4a has "un tiédeur" and "se crispait de lui-même" in 708 words.

The proposed prompt is not a finished answer: it fixed the facts and the ending, not the habits or the length.

### Prompt problems

- **The same rule two or three times in one request. [R]** How to open: three versions. How to end: three, partly contradicting ("never on a reflection" against "an unspoken realisation"; "end on a threat" against "no mid-action cliffhanger"). Emotions: two. No summary: three.
- **Rules for every scene produce every scene alike. [J]** Two or three senses at each opening, emotion always through the body, each attempt a "yes, but", each ending a turn. Followed faithfully, these are the formula.
- **"Écris en French". [R]** `{book.language}` expands to the English word inside the French prompt (`lib/voice.ts`).
- **"Fantasy novelist" is written into** the outline, entry and tension prompts, whatever the book is. [C]
- **"Rewrite" with no direction** asks to "improve the prose", which invites a generic polish. [C]

### Context problems

- **The cast and the place are not looked up. [R]** `buildContext` finds entries by names in the instruction, the selection and the text before the cursor. The card's note, beats, cast and place are not scanned. Your book has 32 voice cards; when a scene is written from its card, only the point-of-view character's is sent, with those of entries linked to it.
- **The story so far depends on summaries nobody makes. [R]** They are created when a scene is marked done, or from the Dashboard. Your book has none. Without them, chapter 2 knows chapter 1 only through 150 words.
- **Scenes of the same chapter do not see each other. [C]** "Write the chapter" gives scene 3 the last 150 words of scene 2 and nothing of scene 1.
- **Summaries of summaries. [C]** Two to four sentences per scene, then a chapter summary made from those, then a paragraph for the book. Plot only: nothing on who knows what, injuries, objects, promises.
- **The chat cannot read the book. [C]** It gets the bible and the style guide: not the outline, the foundation, the manuscript or the open scene. The foundation interview asks about "what is missing" without seeing what is filled.
- **Planning reads short versions only. [C]** A character's goals, secrets and arc are in the full version.

### Voice

- **Samples come only from scenes Claude wrote little of** (25 % or less, counted once and never revised). In a book drafted with Claude there are none, so the voice is the preset. Your three excerpts are the only anchor. [C]
- **"Describe my voice" has no such filter:** without excerpts it reads every non-draft scene, including Claude's. [C]
- **There is no way to say "this chapter is the tone I want".** You made that judgement on the Aurors chapter; Plume cannot record it.

### What I would build

1. **Context by kind of work.**
   - Small edits (rewrite, shorten, describe): as today.
   - Continue and beats: the whole scene so far, the cast's entries, the chapter's outline.
   - Drafting a scene: full entries with voice cards for the cast, the place, every entry named on the card; the chapter so far in full; the end of the previous chapter; the cards before and after; every chapter summary.
   - The cost is small against the reply: run 2 was 3,887 tokens in and 12,169 out.
2. **A story memory that maintains itself.** Summarise a scene when you leave it changed. Keep chapter and book summaries current. Show "memory: 28 of 28" beside the meter.
3. **One voice sheet.** Your reference passages first (any scene or chapter you mark, whoever typed it), then one text of instructions, each rule once. Banned words leave the prompt and stay in the check.
4. **Length as guidance.** A range in the first request, tied to the beats. If the reply is short, say so and offer to expand a beat you pick, with the whole scene in the request. No automatic continuation.
5. **A model policy with three roles:** drafting, editing and planning, small jobs. Each has a model and an effort you can see and change. `fable`, `xhigh` and `max` become available. Editing and planning default to the strongest model you are willing to spend on; they are rare calls. Test on your own chapters before settling.
6. **One way to ask for structure.** The CLI has `--json-schema`; one function for JSON tasks with validation and one retry.
7. **A habits list per book.** A local report of images and moves repeated across scenes (the same simile appeared in two separate runs), shown to you, and given to Claude only as "already used in this book".

## 5. Planning, in depth

### What is there

- **The story:** premise, ten foundation fields, turning points from nine structure templates; in a series, the series plan.
- **The outline:** chapters with goal and ending; scene cards with summary, point of view, place, time, cast, beats, what the reader learns, seeds, arc, pace; locks; ten earlier versions per chapter.
- **Claude:** outline from a premise, detail a chapter or all, regenerate a chapter or a card, revise the whole outline from one instruction, check the outline, fix an issue, create missing entries, propose the foundation, interview, propose turning points, rate tension.
- **Views:** Cards, List, Board, Grid; Analysis with Structure, Timeline, Story lines, Dashboard.

### What works

- **Every Claude change is a proposal** shown beside what exists, with Accept, Reject, Undo and versions. Written scenes, locked cards and published chapters are protected in code, not only in the prompt.
- **"Revise the outline"** returns a list of changes to tick. This is better than what I know of in competing tools.
- **The card is the scene.** No second copy to keep in step.
- **The plan reaches the prose:** note, beats, cast, arc and pace are all in the writing request.

### What does not

- **One doctrine, not editable. [C]** "A chapter has a concrete goal, escalates by yes-but and no-and, ends on a turn; most chapters are one scene." It is a good default and the only one. A writer who plans by character, by theme or loosely has no setting for it, unlike the prose prompt.
- **The chapter length is fixed text. [C]** The rules say "about 3,000 to 4,500 words" whatever the book's setting (yours is 5,000 to 6,000).
- **"Outline from a premise" starts too easily. [C]** It runs on twenty characters with an empty foundation and says nothing. It defaults to 12 chapters for 90,000 words, which contradicts its own chapter length. It asks no questions and offers no alternatives.
- **Pieces that do not talk to each other. [C]**

| Element | Written by Claude's cards? | Read by Claude when planning? | Read when writing? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premise, foundation | Proposed | Yes | Tone only |
| Turning points | Proposed (two places) | Yes | No |
| Card: note, beats, cast, reveals, arc, pace | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Story lines on a scene | No | No | No |
| Story time | No | No | Yes, if you typed it |
| Tension | Separate rating | No | No |
| Threads (web novels) | Separate search | No | No |
| Full bible entries | No | Short version only | Point of view only |

- **Two editors for one card. [C]** Board: note, point of view, status, time, place, story lines. Outline: title, point of view, place, time, cast, note, beats, reveals, seeds, arc, pace.
- **Two fillers for turning points** with different prompts; one asks before replacing, one does not. [C]
- **Check results are not kept. [C]** "Check the outline" lives in memory; agent findings point to scenes by title, and "Scène 1" exists in every chapter.
- **Regenerating a chapter sees the past and the next chapter,** not the rest of the book. [C]
- **Sonnet at medium effort** for every planning call, including the check that is asked to read "at the level of the genre's great novels". [C]

### Does it help writers make better books?

The mechanics do. The checks for cause and effect, setups and payoffs and pace are the right questions. But the easy path is: premise, outline, detail all, entries, write the chapter, fix what's flagged. Each step is reviewable, and each is also about four hundred beats and a hundred entries of Claude's text to read and approve. On that path the plan is Claude's, and the tool asks the author nothing. That is the risk in your question about "unnecessary work": the work is real, and it is reviewing, not deciding. [J]

### A tighter shape

1. **One Story page** above the outline: premise, foundation, turning points, with the interview and Propose as visible buttons. The interview sees what is filled.
2. **Questions before an outline.** From a premise, Claude first offers two or three directions in a paragraph each, and asks what it needs. The full outline comes after you choose.
3. **One card editor,** everywhere, with every field.
4. **Cards carry story lines and time,** and Claude fills them, so the Timeline and the tracker are populated.
5. **One tracker for promises:** story lines, threads, reveals and seeds become "planted here, moved here, paid here".
6. **Planning reads the full entries** of main characters.
7. **A planning style you can read and edit,** like the voice prompt.
8. **Checks land in one list** that stays.

## 6. Editing and manuscript quality

### By level

| Level | What Plume has | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Developmental | "Edit this chapter" (Deep): continuity with the bible, voices, the chapter's plan, pacing. Agents: continuity and structure from summaries; an audit that reads the files. Check the outline. Beta readers. | Chapter by chapter only. The chapter read does not get earlier chapters or summaries, so continuity means "against the bible". Book-level tasks rest on summaries your book does not have. |
| Line | Three paths: Line edit (scene), Edit this chapter, Fix what's flagged. Rewrite as tracked changes. | The mechanics are right; the three differ in ways a user cannot see. |
| Copy | Windows spellcheck or LanguageTool; French and English typography as you type; name spellings against the bible. | Adequate. For French, Antidote is the reference and is not connected. |
| Manuscript | Twenty local reports per scene or chapter; chapter-by-chapter chart; who says what; story-line gaps; timeline flags. | No book-wide repetition, no opening and ending survey, no voice consistency across the book. |
| Experience | Findings to untick, tracked changes, snapshot first, a guard against rewrites. | Strong, and undermined by changes that vanish on navigation and by one-by-one mouse clicks. |

### The three line-edit paths [C]

| | Line edit | Edit this chapter | Fix what's flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knows your voice (style guide, samples) | No | Yes | Yes |
| Tolerates French spaces and apostrophes when matching | No | No | Yes |
| Says how many fixes could not be placed | No | Yes, a count | Yes |
| Refuses a fix that amounts to a rewrite | No | No | Yes |
| Snapshot first | No | Yes | Yes |
| Keeps changes already waiting | No, replaces them | Yes | Yes |

### The French reports [R]

On twelve sentences with no passive and no adverb:

- **"Passive voice 50 %".** Flagged: *s'était levée*, *était bleu*, *était petit*, *était apparu*, *était décédé*, *s'était approché*.
- **"Adverbs 5".** Flagged: *haussement*, *battement*, *claquement*, *frémissement*, *chuchotement*.
- **The instruction built for Claude:** make those sentences active; replace those nouns with stronger verbs.
- **Sentence splitting** cuts "— Tu viens ? demanda-t-il." in two and "M. Dupont" after "M.".
- **Echoes** count a character's name said twice in forty words.

### Metrics as targets [J]

The reports' targets are, in the code's own words, "Plume's estimates for published fiction, not measured norms". The score is the share of reports under target. "Retry fixing these" and "Fix what's flagged" turn those numbers into instructions. You found on the Aurors chapter that the draft with fewer flagged habits was the one you liked less. The detector of habits (contrasts, triads, repeated openers, ", et il", "comme on") is useful as a reader's aid. As a goal it pushes prose toward the average.

### Against the specialist tools

- **ProWritingAid** separates a per-chapter critique from a whole-manuscript analysis and a "virtual beta reader", up to 300,000 words. Plume has the chapter level only.
- **AutoCrit** sells story-level analysis and a reverse outline; reviews disagree on how reliable they are.
- **Grammarly** added French grammar and spelling in late 2025; nothing for fiction.
- **Antidote** is the French reference: a local corrector, style filters, its own rewording.
- **Where Plume is ahead:** it knows the bible, the plan and each character's voice, so it can say "this contradicts chapter 3's fact" or "Marc would not say this". None of the checkers can.

### Closing the gaps

1. Fix the French detection, and stop sending passive and adverb counts to Claude until it is fixed.
2. One editing flow with three named levels: **Read** (a report, no text changed), **Line edit** (tracked changes, in your voice), **Proof** (LanguageTool or Antidote).
3. One engine under it: tolerant matching, the guard, a snapshot of the chapter, changes that persist.
4. Reports stay as information; the score becomes "reports over your target" or goes.
5. A whole-manuscript read (section 9, P3): the book fits in one request on a large-context model.
6. For French, add how characters address each other (tu or vous): the test runs switched between runs because nothing says.

## 8. Workflow and cohesion

| Stage | What flows | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Start a book | Language, fantasy voice applied, a first chapter | The fantasy preset is applied to any book. |
| 2. Concept | Premise and foundation feed every planning request | The interview cannot see the foundation. |
| 3. Plan | Outline, detail, check, revise all read the foundation and the outline so far | Story lines, time and tension are outside the loop. |
| 4. Characters and world | Entries from the chat, the text or the outline land in "To review" | Planning reads short versions only; voice cards sit under "Advanced". |

| 7. Review | Reports highlight in the text | Findings live in six places; French counts are wrong. |
| 8. Edit | Findings become tracked changes | Three paths; changes are lost on navigation; no book level. |

## 9. Roadmap

Complexity: low is under a day, medium a few days, high a week or more. "Kind" separates genuine problems from preferences and options.

### P0: must fix

**P0-1. Switching books loses pending saves** — bug [R]
- **Problem:** `close()` does not wait for `flush()`; later writes find no book.
- **Why:** silent loss of text or plan changes.
- **Fix:** wait for the flush before clearing the book; make each pending write carry its own data; test it.
- **Benefit:** no loss on switch. **Complexity:** low. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** none; add a store test first.

**P0-2. Scenes written from the plan lack their cast and place** — bug [R]
- **Problem:** entries are found from the text near the cursor only.
- **Why:** contradictions with the bible in every test run; voice cards unused.
- **Fix:** also take the card's cast, place, and names in its note and beats; cast in full, with voice cards.
- **Benefit:** the largest single gain in consistency seen in the runs. **Complexity:** low. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** bible budget; raise it for this action.

**P0-3. The length follow-up adds a second ending** — writing-quality fault [R]
- **Problem:** under 85 % of the floor, a second request continues a finished scene from its last 700 words.
- **Why:** invented plot beyond the card, twice in two tests; triggers at your book's length.
- **Fix:** remove it. Ask for a range tied to the beats; if short, report it and offer to expand a chosen beat.
- **Benefit:** scenes end once, on the plan. **Complexity:** low to remove, medium for "expand a beat". **Change:** existing, plus one small new action.
- **Risks:** shorter first drafts; that is the honest result.

**P0-4. The French scorecard reports faults that are not there** — bug [R]
- **Problem:** passive and adverb detection by word shape.
- **Why:** wrong numbers on every French scene, turned into rewrite instructions.
- **Fix:** a -ment word after a determiner is a noun; exclude pronominal verbs and adjectives or drop the French passive check; fix sentence splitting on dialogue; exclude bible names from echoes. Until then, keep both out of what is sent to Claude.
- **Benefit:** reports you can trust. **Complexity:** medium. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** French grammar by rules stays approximate; say so in the report.

**P0-5. A failed save does not stop the close** — bug [C]
- **Problem:** the close handler ignores a failed flush; the warning fades.
- **Why:** everything since the failure is lost without a prompt.
- **Fix:** block the close, name the files, offer retry or "save a copy elsewhere"; a bar that stays while a save is failing.
- **Benefit:** no silent loss. **Complexity:** low. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** none.

**P0-6. Tracked changes and proposals vanish on navigation** — significant UX fault [R]
- **Problem:** they exist only in the open editor.
- **Why:** a paid chapter edit is lost by visiting the Plan.
- **Fix:** keep them per scene in the app's state and in `.plume/`; restore when the scene opens; drop a change whose words are gone.
- **Benefit:** review at your own pace. **Complexity:** medium. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** positions drift if the text changes; anchor by quote, as comments do.

### P1: high priority

**P1-1. A story memory and context by kind of work** — quality problem [R][C]
- **Problem:** no summaries unless asked; 150 words of the previous scene; a 1,500-word window for everything.
- **Why:** continuity is the reason to use Plume over a chat.
- **Fix:** automatic summaries; the chapter so far and the previous chapter's end when drafting; three context levels.
- **Benefit:** fewer contradictions, steadier voice. **Complexity:** medium. **Change:** existing, plus an automatic job.
- **Risks:** small background calls on Haiku; show them and allow turning off.

**P1-2. Each rule once** — quality problem [R][J]
- **Problem:** 26 bullets with duplicates and contradictions; a ban list that misfires; "French"; "fantasy novelist".
- **Why:** conflicting rules are followed unevenly; universal rules make scenes alike.
- **Fix:** one voice sheet; scene-level intent from the card; banned words in the check only; genre from the book.
- **Benefit:** a prompt you can read and reason about. **Complexity:** medium. **Change:** existing prompts.
- **Risks:** prose changes; compare on your chapters before and after. The sample in `audit-samples/4-…` is a starting point, not a result.

**P1-3. A model policy that works** — genuine problem [R]
- **Problem:** dead settings, fixed effort, Sonnet hard-coded for analysis, no access to `fable` or higher effort.
- **Why:** the rare, hard calls (checking a whole outline, editing a chapter) run on the cheaper setting with no way to change it.
- **Fix:** three roles with model and effort; remove presets and per-action leftovers; keep the OpenRouter fallback.
- **Benefit:** control, and one place to look. **Complexity:** medium. **Change:** existing code, removing more than adding.
- **Risks:** usage limits; show the role's cost in the meter.

**P1-4. A voice reference you choose** — quality problem [C][J]
- **Problem:** samples exist only for scenes Claude barely touched.
- **Why:** on a Claude-drafted book the voice is the preset.
- **Fix:** "Use as voice reference" on any scene or chapter; excerpts first, then references; "Describe my voice" reads the same set.
- **Benefit:** the tone you approved carries forward. **Complexity:** low. **Change:** existing code plus one flag.
- **Risks:** a loop where Claude imitates Claude; it is your approval that breaks it, so keep it explicit.

**P1-5. The chat can read the book** — genuine gap [C]
- **Problem:** bible and style guide only.
- **Why:** it sits beside an outline and a scene it cannot see.
- **Fix:** add the foundation, the outline (compact), the open scene and the summaries, within a budget.
- **Benefit:** a brainstorm partner that knows the story. **Complexity:** low. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** larger requests; cap and show.

**P1-6. One editing flow** — genuine problem [C]
- **Problem:** three line-edit paths, plus scene checks and agent tasks that overlap.
- **Why:** different results for the same intent; the weakest path has the plainest name.
- **Fix:** Read, Line edit, Proof; one engine; keys and "accept the rest" for changes.
- **Benefit:** predictable editing. **Complexity:** medium. **Change:** existing code merged.
- **Risks:** removing buttons people learned; map old to new in the "what moved" card.

**P1-7. Reports as information** — partly preference [J]
- **Problem:** estimated targets become a score and then instructions.
- **Why:** it steers prose toward an average; your own test went that way.
- **Fix:** keep highlights and counts; score becomes "over your target" or goes; "Retry fixing" stays for banned words and habits you chose, not for rates.
- **Benefit:** the author decides. **Complexity:** low. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** you may like the score; then keep it and drop only the automatic fixing of rates.

**P1-8. Connect the plan** — genuine problem [C]
- **Problem:** two card editors, duplicate turning-point filler, fixed chapter length, story lines and time outside Claude's cards, an outline dialog with no guard.
- **Why:** half the Analysis tabs stay empty; fields depend on which view you are in.
- **Fix:** the list in section 5, items 1, 3, 4 and the dialog guard.
- **Benefit:** one plan. **Complexity:** medium. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** none.

**P1-9. Typing speed on long books** — genuine problem [R]
- **Problem:** work per keystroke grows with the book.
- **Why:** it will be felt at novel length, more in Review.
- **Fix:** keep per-scene counts until the text changes; subscribe panes to what they show; run reports after a pause.
- **Benefit:** flat typing cost. **Complexity:** low to medium. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** stale counts for a moment.

**P1-10. Backups that cannot drop to one** — genuine problem [C]
- **Problem:** age-only pruning; keyed by title.
- **Why:** the backup is the last resort.
- **Fix:** always keep the last 10 plus one a week; key by the book's folder or an id.
- **Benefit:** a real safety net. **Complexity:** low. **Change:** existing code.
- **Risks:** disk use, small.

## 10. Final assessment

1. **Is the architecture good enough?** Yes. Plain files, a small native side and tested pure logic suit this product. The faults are local: the save lifecycle, state kept inside editor views, an AI layer without a common shape. None needs a rewrite.
2. **Is the feature set balanced?** No. Breadth is far ahead of proof. Your own roadmap says 1.0 needs every feature used on a real book; your real book uses a small part, and the part it uses most (plan, bible, drafting) has the P0s.
3. **Is the UX efficient for professionals?** The writing loop is. Around it, actions are hidden, the same thing lives in several places, and review work can be lost.
4. **Is the Plan as intelligent and streamlined as it could be?** The mechanics are excellent. It is not yet a system: trackers Claude never fills, two editors for one card, one doctrine, and a path that makes the author a reviewer.
5. **Can the AI give consistently exceptional help?** Not yet. It is given too little of the book and too many rules, at the cheapest settings, with two mechanical loops that hurt the text. The runs show the facts problem is fixable with what Plume already stores.
6. **Is editing competitive?** In how changes are shown and controlled, yes, better than most. In French accuracy and at manuscript level, no.
7. **Can Plume help produce publication-ready books?** It can carry a disciplined author from plan to a revised draft. The final proofread and print formatting still need other tools.
8. **Biggest weaknesses:** thin and unreliable story memory; over-specified prompts; metrics used as targets; work lost at the edges of a session; breadth unverified in real use.
9. **Strongest advantages:** your files, your subscription; a bible that controls what the AI reads; proposals and tracked changes everywhere; dated facts, voice cards and point-of-view knowledge; French as a first language.
10. **The ten most important improvements:** P0-1 to P0-6, then P1-1 (story memory), P1-2 (each rule once), P1-3 (model policy), P1-6 (one editing flow).

### The ideal Plume

A quiet page, and behind it three things that are always true and always current: what the world is (the bible), what should happen (the plan), and what has happened (the story memory). Every request to Claude is built from those three plus passages you approved as your voice, and you can see it before it is sent. Claude asks before it outlines, proposes instead of applying, and stops when the card is played. Editing has three named levels, and every change waits for you, wherever you go in the app. The reports tell you what is there and leave the judgement to you. Nothing is lost by closing, switching or looking elsewhere. It does fewer things than today's Plume, and each of them has been used on a real book.
